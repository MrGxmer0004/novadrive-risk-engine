How the scores and the model work

X: supplier reliability, 0 to 1

X measures how dependable a supplier is on its own. It is built from the Supplier Indicators sheet with fixed thresholds, so no score depends on the rest of the sample.

X = 0.30 × Financial + 0.30 × Operational + 0.20 × Geography + 0.20 × Assurance Financial = mean of current ratio (0.8 → 0, 2.0 → 1) and net debt/EBITDA (6x → 0, 1x → 1) Operational = mean of 3-month timeliness (75% → 0, 95% → 1) and change since January (-10 pts → 0, +2 pts → 1) Geography = 1 − mean of hazard, logistics and infrastructure indices / 100 Assurance = 1 − assurance gap index / 100

E: supply efficiency, the share of planned output a chain can be relied on to deliver

A supplier is only as dependable as its weakest required input. Each upstream link carries a dependency d: 1.0 where the evidence confirms a sole source, the slider value (0.60 by default) where exclusivity is not stated.

Input assurance S = 1 − d × (1 − E of the input supplier) Supplier E = min( own X, S of every input ) Component E = sum over Tier-1 suppliers of allocation share × E Product E = min over its components Total efficiency = revenue-weighted mean of product E

Losing a supplier

A lost supplier has E = 0. The model recomputes every supplier, component and product. Revenue at risk is each product's revenue times the fraction of its efficiency that is lost. Upstream suppliers are listed with the share of their mapped customers that disappears.

Compensating

For each lost supplier the tool finds the lowest X a replacement needs for total efficiency to return to the baseline, then ranks the researched alternates: those that meet the threshold first, then by fitment. Where a second source is already qualified, shifting volume to it is offered first. A replacement is assumed to arrive with its own upstream chain, which is not mapped.

Limits

  • No spend, inventory or capacity data exists, so results are exposure estimates, not forecasts.
  • Tier-2 and Tier-3 qualification times are not given; the lead time of the component they feed is used as a floor.
  • X for real alternates is a coarse estimate from public profiles and is not comparable in precision to X from workbook indicators.
  • All case entities are fictional. Public sources are used only for alternates.
